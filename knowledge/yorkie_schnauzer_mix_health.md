## Yorkie-Schnauzer mix overview
A Yorkie-Schnauzer mix is a small dog with a wiry or silky coat and a lively, alert personality. Common health themes from the parent breeds include dental disease, pancreatitis, bladder stones, diabetes, eye problems such as cataracts, a sensitive windpipe, and luxating kneecaps. A mixed dog may have some, all, or none of these.
Tip: Ask the vet which parent-breed conditions to watch for in a Yorkie-Schnauzer mix and screen for them at checkups.

## Pancreatitis and fatty foods
Miniature Schnauzers are prone to high blood fat and pancreatitis. Signs include vomiting, belly pain, loss of appetite, and lethargy. Avoid fatty human food, and introduce any diet change slowly.
Tip: Avoid fatty foods and treats for Schnauzer-type dogs, and call the vet if vomiting or belly pain appears.

## Harness instead of collar
Yorkies can have a weak or collapsing windpipe. A harness avoids pressure on the neck. A honking cough, especially when excited or pulling on a leash, should be reported to the vet.
Tip: Walk Yorkie-type dogs on a harness rather than a collar, and report any honking cough.

## Bladder stones and water
Schnauzers are prone to bladder stones. Fresh water at all times and regular chances to urinate help. Straining to urinate, blood in urine, or frequent small urinations need a vet visit.
Tip: Keep fresh water available and offer frequent bathroom breaks, since Schnauzer-type dogs are prone to bladder stones.

## Dental disease in small breeds
Toy and small breeds crowd their teeth, so tartar and gum disease build up fast. Brushing and professional cleanings are the main prevention.
Tip: Brush small-breed dog teeth several times a week.

## What counts as senior
Small dogs are usually considered seniors from about 10 years old, and larger dogs earlier. Senior dogs benefit from more frequent vet checkups, often every six months, with bloodwork and a dental exam.

## Slowing down
Older dogs tire faster, sleep more, and recover more slowly after activity. Keep routines predictable and keep exercise gentle and regular instead of occasional and intense.
Tip: Keep senior dogs on a steady daily routine with gentle exercise rather than occasional long outings.

## Signs to report to the vet
Report changes in appetite, drinking, urination, weight, breathing, mobility, or behavior. Confusion at night, pacing, or forgetting routines can be signs of canine cognitive dysfunction and are worth raising with the vet.
Tip: Note any changes in appetite, thirst, weight, mobility, or behavior and tell the vet.

## Senior nutrition
Seniors need to hold a healthy weight. Extra weight stresses joints and heart, while unexplained weight loss is a warning sign. Ask the vet whether a senior diet suits your dog.
Tip: Weigh senior dogs regularly and ask the vet about diet if weight changes.

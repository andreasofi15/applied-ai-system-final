## Who Chester is
Chester is a 12-year-old (born 2014) Yorkie-Schnauzer mix, sometimes called a Schnorkie. He is a small senior dog. Mixed breeds can inherit health tendencies from both parent breeds, so Chester's care combines Yorkie and Miniature Schnauzer considerations.
Tip: Plan Chester's day like a small senior dog: gentle, shorter, more frequent activity, steady meals, and regular vet checkups.

## Chester's walks
Chester does best with short, gentle walks on a harness instead of a collar, because Yorkie ancestry brings a risk of a sensitive windpipe. Several short walks are easier on a 12-year-old than one long walk. Slow down or stop if he lags, pants heavily, coughs, or limps.

Tip: Keep Chester's walks to about 15-20 minutes, use a harness, and shorten them in heat or cold or if he slows down.

## Chester's feeding
The Schnauzer side of Chester's family is prone to pancreatitis, which is often triggered by fatty foods. Keep meals consistent and avoid fatty table scraps, bacon, and greasy treats. Feed measured portions at the same times each day.
Tip: Give Chester measured meals at consistent times and skip fatty scraps and rich treats to protect his pancreas.

## Chester's teeth
Yorkies and Schnauzers are both prone to dental disease, and at 12 Chester is at higher risk of tooth and gum problems. Daily or near-daily tooth brushing with dog toothpaste helps, along with regular dental checks at the vet.

Tip: Brush Chester's teeth with dog toothpaste several times a week, and have the vet check his mouth at each visit.

## Chester's comfort and joints
Small senior dogs can develop stiff joints and slippery-floor trouble. Soft bedding, rugs on hard floors, and ramps or steps to furniture help. Yorkies are also prone to luxating kneecaps, so watch for skipping or hopping on a back leg.
Tip: Give Chester non-slip floors and a soft bed, and mention any skipping or limping to the vet.
